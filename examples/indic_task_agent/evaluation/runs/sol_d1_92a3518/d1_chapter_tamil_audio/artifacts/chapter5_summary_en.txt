Chapter Five, The Journey of a Water Drop, explains how water moves continuously through nature. Powered by the sun's heat and gravity, this journey has no beginning or end.

First, sunlight warms oceans, lakes, and rivers, turning some water into invisible water vapor. This is evaporation. Plants also release vapor through their leaves, a process called transpiration.

As moist air rises and cools, water vapor condenses into tiny droplets that gather to form clouds. When these droplets grow heavy, water returns to Earth as rain, snow, or hail. This is precipitation. India's southwest monsoon brings much of its rainfall between June and September.

Rainwater flows into streams, rivers, lakes, and eventually the sea. Some soaks into the soil, replenishing groundwater that supplies wells and springs and supports farming.

The water cycle provides fresh water, influences weather, and moves heat around the planet. Only about three percent of Earth's water is freshwater, and much of it is frozen. Forests help return moisture to the atmosphere.

We can protect this precious resource by closing taps while brushing, repairing leaks, harvesting rainwater, and reusing vegetable-washing water for plants. The chapter's central message is simple: water keeps circulating, but usable freshwater is limited, so every drop deserves care.
