This chapter explains the water cycle, describing how a single drop of water travels through various stages on Earth. It begins with evaporation, where sunlight heats water bodies and turns water into invisible vapor. Plants also release vapor through transpiration. The vapor rises, cools, and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds (condensation). When cloud droplets become heavy enough, they fall as rain, hail, or drizzle, depending on temperature. Rainwater collects in streams, rivers, and eventually returns to the sea, while some infiltrates the ground to become groundwater, stored in wells and aquifers. The chapter emphasizes the importance of the water cycle for drinking water, agriculture, and industry, noting that only a small fraction of Earth's water is fresh and usable. It also highlights how forests affect rainfall and the need to conserve water by fixing leaks, collecting rainwater, and reusing household water. The section ends with exercises and questions to reinforce understanding of evaporation, cloud formation, precipitation forms, groundwater importance, and practical water‑saving measures.
