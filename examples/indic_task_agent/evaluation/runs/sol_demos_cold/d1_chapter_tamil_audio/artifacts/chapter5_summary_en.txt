Chapter Five, The Journey of a Water Drop, explains how water continuously moves through nature. This water cycle has no beginning or end. The sun's heat and gravity keep it moving.

First, sunlight heats water in oceans, rivers, and lakes, turning some of it into invisible water vapor. This is evaporation. Plants also release water vapor through their leaves, a process called transpiration.

As moist air rises, it cools. Water vapor condenses into tiny droplets that gather around dust particles and form clouds. When these droplets combine and become heavy, water falls as rain, snow, or hail. This is precipitation.

Rainwater flows into streams and rivers and eventually returns to the sea. Some seeps into the soil, becoming groundwater that supplies wells and springs. Lakes and ponds store water for use during dry seasons.

The water cycle supplies drinking water, supports farming and industry, and influences weather. Only about three percent of Earth's water is freshwater, so conservation matters. Cutting forests can also reduce local rainfall.

We can help by closing taps while brushing, repairing leaks, collecting rainwater, and reusing vegetable-washing water for plants. The chapter's central message is simple: water connects all life, and every drop deserves protection.
