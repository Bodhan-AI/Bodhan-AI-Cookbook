Chapter Five, The Journey of a Water Drop, explains how water continually travels through nature. This endless water cycle is powered by the sun's heat and gravity.

First comes evaporation. Sunlight warms oceans, rivers, and lakes, turning some water into invisible water vapor. Plants also release water vapor through their leaves, a process called transpiration.

As moist air rises, it cools. Water vapor condenses into tiny droplets that gather around dust particles and form clouds. When the droplets grow heavy enough, water returns to Earth as rain, snow, or hail. This is precipitation. India's southwest monsoon brings much of its rainfall between June and September.

Rainwater collects in rivers, lakes, and ponds, or seeps into the ground. Groundwater supplies wells and springs, while stored water helps farmers during dry periods. Eventually, water returns to the sea, and its journey continues.

The cycle supports drinking water, farming, industry, and weather. Only about three percent of Earth's water is freshwater, and much of it is frozen. Forests also help return moisture to the air.

We can protect this precious resource by closing taps while brushing, fixing leaks, harvesting rainwater, and reusing vegetable-washing water for plants. Every drop matters.
