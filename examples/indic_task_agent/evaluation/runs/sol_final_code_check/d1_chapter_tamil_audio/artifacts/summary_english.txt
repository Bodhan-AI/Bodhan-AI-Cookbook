Chapter Five, The Journey of a Water Drop, explains how water moves continuously around Earth. Powered by the sun's heat and gravity, the water cycle has no beginning or end.

First, sunlight warms oceans, lakes, and rivers, turning some water into invisible water vapor. This is evaporation. Plants also release water vapor through their leaves, a process called transpiration.

As moist air rises and cools, water vapor condenses into tiny droplets around dust particles, forming clouds. When these droplets grow heavy, water returns to Earth as rain, snow, or hail. This is precipitation. In India, the southwest monsoon brings much of the year's rainfall.

Rainwater collects in ponds, lakes, streams, and rivers, eventually returning to the sea. Some seeps into the ground, replenishing groundwater that supplies wells and springs and supports farming.

The water cycle provides freshwater, influences weather, and distributes heat. Only about three percent of Earth's water is freshwater, much of it frozen, so conservation matters. Forests also help return moisture to the atmosphere.

We can protect water by closing taps while brushing, repairing leaks, collecting rainwater, and reusing vegetable-washing water for plants. The chapter's central message is simple: water keeps circulating, but usable freshwater is precious. Every drop deserves care.
