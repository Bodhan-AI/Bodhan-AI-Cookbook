Chapter 5 explores the fascinating journey of a water drop through the endless water cycle, driven by the sun's heat and gravity. Every drop of water on Earth is ancient, having traveled across oceans, rivers, and ice caps. The cycle begins with evaporation, where the sun turns water from oceans, lakes, and rivers into water vapor, while plants release water through transpiration. As warm, moist air rises, it cools down and condenses around dust particles to form clouds. When these droplets become too heavy, they fall back to the earth as precipitation, such as rain, snow, or hail. This rainwater is collected in rivers, lakes, and underground as groundwater, sustaining agriculture and supplying our drinking water. Because only three percent of Earth's water is freshwater, conserving every drop is vital. Simple actions like turning off taps while brushing teeth, harvesting rainwater, and fixing leaks help preserve this precious resource for the future.
