Chapter Four: Water and Life.

About seventy percent of the Earth is covered by water, but most of it is salt water in the oceans. Only a small part is fresh water that we can use.

The water cycle moves water between the ocean, the air and the land. The sun heats the water and it rises as vapour. This is evaporation. The vapour cools and forms clouds. This is condensation. When the drops become heavy, they fall as rain, snow or hail. This is precipitation. In India, most rain falls during the monsoon, from June to September. Plants also release water vapour through their leaves. This is transpiration.

Rainwater flows into rivers or soaks into the ground as groundwater, which farmers draw from wells. Rivers like the Ganga, the Godavari and the Kaveri have supported farming and cities for thousands of years.

Clean water is essential for health. Dirty water spreads cholera and typhoid. Boiling, filtering or adding chlorine makes water safe.

Many places face water shortages in summer, when wells dry up. We can help by harvesting rainwater, using drip irrigation, turning off taps while brushing, and fixing leaks. Fresh water is precious, so everyone must use it wisely.
