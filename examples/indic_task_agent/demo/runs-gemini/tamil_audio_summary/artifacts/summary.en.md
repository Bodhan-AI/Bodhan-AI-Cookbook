This chapter covers the basic principles of lines and angles in geometry. A line segment has two endpoints, a ray has one endpoint, and a line extends indefinitely. Three or more points lying on the same straight line are called collinear points.

An angle is formed when two rays originate from a common vertex. Angles are classified based on their measures: acute angles are between 0 and 90 degrees, right angles equal 90 degrees, obtuse angles are between 90 and 180 degrees, straight angles equal 180 degrees, and reflex angles are between 180 and 360 degrees. Two angles whose sum is 90 degrees are complementary, while two angles whose sum is 180 degrees are supplementary.

Adjacent angles share a common vertex and a common arm. When their non-common arms form a straight line, they create a linear pair, and their sum is equal to 180 degrees. This is known as the Linear Pair Axiom. Conversely, if the sum of two adjacent angles is 180 degrees, their non-common arms form a straight line. Finally, when two straight lines intersect each other at a point, the vertically opposite angles formed are equal. These basic properties are essential for solving geometric problems.
