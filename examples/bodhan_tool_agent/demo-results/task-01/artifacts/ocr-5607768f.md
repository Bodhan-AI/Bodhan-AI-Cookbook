The Little Lantern

A short story about kindness, courage, and the light we share

In a quiet village surrounded by green hills, there lived a boy named Aarav. Every evening, he helped
his grandfather light the lanterns along the narrow road that led to the river. The old lanterns were small,
but their warm glow helped travellers find their way home.

One rainy evening, a strong wind swept through the village. It blew out every lantern and covered the
road in darkness. Aarav looked outside and noticed a stranger standing near the old bridge. The man
seemed frightened and unsure which way to go. Aarav wanted to help, but the storm was getting worse.

“Take my lantern,” his grandfather said, handing him the last one that was still burning. “A little light
can make a big difference.” Aarav held the lantern close and stepped carefully into the rain. He walked
beside the stranger, guiding him away from the slippery bridge and towards the village shelter.

When they arrived, the stranger thanked Aarav warmly. He explained that he was a teacher travelling to
a nearby town. The storm had made him lose his way, and without the lantern he might have been in
danger. Aarav felt proud, not because he had done something extraordinary, but because he had been
able to help.

The next morning, the villagers gathered to repair the lanterns. The teacher joined them and taught the
children how to make simple lamps from jars and candles. Soon, every home had an extra lantern ready
for a neighbour in need. The road looked brighter than ever before.

That evening, Aarav and his grandfather watched the lights shine across the village. “Now you
understand,” his grandfather said, “that light is not only something we carry. It is also something we
give.” Aarav smiled as he watched the little lanterns glow in the darkness.
