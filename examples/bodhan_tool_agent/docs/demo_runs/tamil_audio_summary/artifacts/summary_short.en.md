Summary of Chapter 3: How Plants Make Food (Class 7 Science)

Green plants create their own food by photosynthesis. Leaves take in carbon dioxide from the air and water from the roots, while sunlight provides the energy. The green pigment chlorophyll captures sunlight and drives the reaction that turns carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.

Photosynthesis mainly occurs in leaf cells that have many tiny pores called stomata, which open in daylight to let in carbon dioxide and close at night to save water.

Roots absorb water and minerals, which travel up through xylem tubes to the leaves. Tall trees can move water over 100 metres.

The overall equation is:

    carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Plants store excess glucose as starch in roots, stems, and seeds – foods like potatoes and rice are rich in starch.

This process is the base of most food chains: animals eat plants, and other animals eat those animals. Plants also release the oxygen we breathe.
