The chapter explains the water cycle, a continuous process that moves water around the Earth. Solar energy drives the cycle by causing water to evaporate from oceans, rivers, lakes, and even from plant leaves through transpiration. The water vapor rises, cools, and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds. When these droplets combine and become heavy enough, they fall as precipitation—rain, snow, or hail—returning water to the land. The water then collects in streams, rivers, and groundwater, eventually flowing back to the oceans, where the cycle begins again. The chapter highlights the importance of the monsoon in India, providing essential rainfall for agriculture, especially rice cultivation. It also mentions that groundwater is accessed through wells and handpumps, and that the cycle is essential for sustaining life and ecosystems. By understanding each stage—evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, and collection—students can appreciate how water moves through the environment and why conserving water resources is crucial.
